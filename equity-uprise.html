Policy intelligence

Write the standard. Then make it usable.

The DeKalb data-center work is designed as an administrative reliability package: objective triggers, measurable operating requirements, predictable review, public summaries, due process, and continuing compliance.

DeKalb primary caseNortheast comparisonRed-team reviewed

The framework treats scale as a policy choice that must be justified rather than pretending a single threshold is universally correct. Anti-segmentation rules aggregate related phases, buildings, common control, contiguous tracts, and shared substations so a large project cannot evade review through paperwork.

Traditional nuisance language can miss persistent low-frequency and tonal conditions. The proposed architecture uses measurable acoustic protocols, baseline conditions, verification after operation, corrective action, and defined review rather than relying on subjective complaints after construction.

Water review links proposed demand to actual watershed and utility capacity. Separate metering and Water Usage Effectiveness reporting turn cooling impacts into auditable operating data, while drought-response obligations prevent the permit from assuming unlimited future supply.

The work separates true emergency backup from routine generation and peak-shaving behavior, then asks the correct jurisdictional question: what belongs in local land-use review, what belongs with the utility/PSC, and what requires state or federal air permitting.

Independent review is only credible when the process itself is specified. The framework addresses who selects the reviewer, how conflicts are handled, how applicant-funded escrow works, what the reviewer can examine, and how the applicant responds on the public record.

A permit should not become unreviewable the day a facility opens. The model ties approval to commissioning, operating verification, material-change review, notice and cure, emergency authority, retesting, written determinations, and appeal procedures.
